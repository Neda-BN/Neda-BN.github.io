Overview

The problem

Administrators sent healthcare quality surveys to social workers across many municipalities, using Outlook, Excel and shared folders. Nobody could see who had answered, and reports took weeks.

What I did

Interviewed 4 quality managers, mapped the whole survey workflow, designed the information architecture and the platform, and tested it with 5 administrators. I also built 100% of the front-end myself with Cursor.

Key decisions

  • A six-step wizard for sending surveys
  • A hierarchy that opens one level at a time

A production-ready, WCAG-compliant platform in under eight weeks. In testing, creating and sending a survey went from about 2 hours to about 15 minutes. The platform has launched with two customers and is used in several Swedish municipalities.

Introduction

The challenge

In Sweden, healthcare quality surveys, for example for HVB and LSS services, go to social workers across many municipalities. Administrators created, sent, chased and reported on them in Outlook, Excel and shared folders: emails by hand, a spreadsheet per municipality, missed deadlines, no overview of who had answered, and quarterly reports that took up to two weeks.

The platform started as an idea from my manager at Focalpay and became its own B2B SaaS product. Our goal was to cut administrators' workload by an estimated 40%, without lowering data quality.

The one question

So every decision had to answer one question:

can a busy administrator send a survey without missing anyone?

My role

  • Sole designer, leading UX and UI end to end, from research to final screens.
  • Working closely with the PM, stakeholders and the back-end team.
  • I built the whole front-end myself with Cursor, while the front-end team was busy with another project.
  • Designed to WCAG guidelines across mobile, tablet and desktop.
Discover

What we found

I wanted to understand how administrators actually work, not how the process looks on paper. I combined interviews with 4 quality managers, workflow mapping, a document review and observation. Four findings shaped the work:

  1. Chasing replies took more time than analysing care. "I spend more time chasing people for replies than actually analysing the quality of care." — Interviews · 4 quality managers
  2. Every step happened in a different tool. Creating, sending, tracking and analysing surveys had nothing connecting them. — Workflow mapping
  3. Spreadsheets were doing far more than they were made for, full of manual formatting and notes. — Document review
  4. Administrators lost track while jumping between tools, and sometimes had to start their tracking again. — Observation

The high points were the guided steps with clear feedback. The low points were choosing recipients by hand and waiting for answers without knowing who had replied.

Turning point

The front-end team was busy. So I built it myself.

The situation

The front-end team was busy with another project

The decision

Build the front-end myself, with their reviews

The front-end team was fully booked on another project. Without someone free to build it, the designs would have stayed in Figma.

So I built the whole front-end myself with Cursor, while the back-end team built the server side, setting and checking every spacing value, colour, state and interaction against exact tokens and accessibility requirements.

The front-end team reviewed my code at every phase, and their feedback taught me a lot. Whenever testing found an issue, a bug came up, or users' feedback called for a change, I made it myself, without waiting for a handoff.

Design

Three decisions

Decision 1 · Sending a survey in six checked steps

Missing just one municipality could ruin a whole reporting round. So I split sending into six steps, each checked before the next one opens.

01

Choose survey

Pick the template to send.

02

Choose recipients

Find and select municipalities in the hierarchy.

03

Set language

Choose the language for each municipality or region.

04

Set deadline

Choose the answer deadline for each group.

05

Review

Check all the details before sending.

06

Send

Send to all chosen recipients at once.

Decision 2 · A hierarchy that opens one level at a time

Showing every administrative level at once created long, impenetrable lists. So the platform shows one level at a time.

LevelDescriptionDesign solution
ProviderTop-level healthcare organisationAlways visible in the sidebar, which can be collapsed
MunicipalityMany per providerOpens with one tap to show its districts
DistrictSub-units within each municipalityOpens inside the municipality, one level at a time
Social WorkerIndividual survey respondentsOnly shown when a district is selected

In testing, most participants missed the Select All button: it had low contrast and sat in the wrong place, so they selected districts one by one. I moved it, made it stand out and added a counter. All 5 participants found it straight away.

Decision 3 · Traffic-light colours instead of number tables

Managers needed to see quickly how each municipality was doing, and big number tables are tiring under time pressure. So I designed a three-colour scoring system that shows status at a glance.

StatusMeaningAction
Good performanceAbove target on all key indicators.No action needed.
Needs attentionClose to the limit, with a risk of falling below target.Keep an eye on it and plan a follow-up.
CriticalBelow the acceptable level.Needs action now.

Reflection

What I learned

  • Show complex structures step by step. I didn't remove levels; I showed one at a time, so each step felt easy.
  • Guided steps help people feel safe. Administrators are careful because mistakes are serious, and a wizard that checks each step gave them confidence.
  • What I'd do differently: I'd agree with the first customers on how to measure workload before launch, by tracking time per survey cycle from day one.
